## Overview

Once you connect an identity provider (IdP), your IdP decides who can use Bifrost and what they get. This page explains how that works day to day: what happens when someone signs in, when your IdP pushes a change over SCIM, when someone's role or group is removed, and when someone leaves.

Customers are the one exception. You manage them in Bifrost, and users reach them through their teams and business units.

If you are setting up a provider for the first time, start with [User Provisioning](/enterprise/user-provisioning) and your [IdP setup guide](/enterprise/user-provisioning#supported-identity-providers).

---

## The three ways changes arrive

| Channel | When it runs | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sign-in** | Every time a user signs in, and when their session refreshes | Reads the claims in the user's sign-in token and applies your mapping rules. |
| **SCIM push** | In real time, whenever your IdP sends a change | Applies the user attributes and group memberships your IdP pushes. |
| **Background sync** | On a schedule (only when SCIM Provisioning is off) | Re-reads users from your IdP so changes land even for people who haven't signed in. |

You choose how sign-in and SCIM share the work with the **claims sync mode**, described in [Choosing a sync mode](#choosing-a-sync-mode).

---

## Choosing a sync mode

| Mode | Shown as | Who decides | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SCIM Provisioning off** | - | Sign-in claims, plus background sync | IdPs without SCIM, or setups that don't need real-time push |
| **SCIM Provisioning on: both** | SCIM and login claims (default) | Sign-in claims **and** SCIM. Whichever ran last wins. | IdPs that send the same user data over SCIM and in the sign-in token, such as when moving onto SCIM gradually |
| **SCIM Provisioning on: SCIM only** | SCIM only - ignore login claims | **SCIM alone.** Sign-in ignores the token's claims and re-applies your mapping rules to what SCIM last sent. | Organizations that want one source of truth, with deprovisioning fully owned by SCIM |

<Warning>
	In **both** mode, your IdP's SCIM push and its sign-in token must agree. Any attribute or group you send over SCIM should also
	appear in the sign-in claims, with the same values. Bifrost keeps one stored copy of each user's attributes and doesn't merge
	the two: a SCIM push overwrites what the token sent, and the next sign-in overwrites what SCIM sent. A mismatch makes a user's
	role, teams, or business units flip each time they sign in and each time your IdP pushes. If you can't keep the two in sync,
	use **SCIM only**.
</Warning>

What changes between the modes:

| | SCIM Provisioning off | Both | SCIM only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A user who hasn't been provisioned yet signs in | Created on the spot | Created on the spot | **Refused** until your IdP pushes them over SCIM |
| Sign-in updates role, teams, business units, profiles | Yes, from the token claims | Yes, from the token claims | Yes, from what SCIM last sent. Token claims are ignored. |
| Sign-in can remove a membership SCIM created | - | Yes, when the token sends a new value for an attribute your rules map | Only when you've changed your mapping rules since SCIM's last push. Team and business unit memberships from SCIM groups are kept. |
| Background sync runs | Yes | No | No |
| What can remove a user | Background sync, or a failed session refresh when your IdP reports the user inactive | SCIM, or a failed session refresh when your IdP reports the user inactive | **SCIM only** |

## What happens at sign-in

Every time someone signs in, Bifrost:

1. **Verifies the sign-in** with your IdP.
2. **Finds the user**, or creates them if they're new (not in SCIM-only mode).
3. **Combines the token with what it remembers.** Bifrost keeps a memory of the last value your IdP sent for each attribute. If this token **leaves an attribute out**, the remembered value is used. If the token **includes the attribute**, even as an empty value, the new value replaces the old one. This stops a slimmed-down token from accidentally stripping someone's access, while still letting your IdP remove access deliberately.
4. **Decides the user's role** from your attribute mappings for roles. If no role can be decided, the sign-in is refused (see below).
5. **Updates teams, business units, projects, and access profiles** from your mapping rules.

In **SCIM only** mode the token's claims are ignored: steps 3 to 5 use what SCIM last sent for the user instead.

### When a sign-in is refused

There is **no default role** in Bifrost, not even for the very first user. Every sign-in must resolve to a role, or Bifrost refuses it. A refused sign-in never deletes an account, and a new user who is refused is not created.

Whether a returning user keeps their role depends on why the sign-in was refused:

| Situation | Signed in? | The user's current role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The token (or memory) has a role attribute that matches an attribute mapping for roles | Yes | Set from the matching mapping |
| You have no attribute mappings for roles, the IdP sends no role claim, and the user already has a role | Yes | **Kept** |
| Neither the token nor memory has any attribute your attribute mappings for roles look at | **Refused** | **Kept.** The token is not applied, so nothing about the user changes. |
| The token has a role attribute, but its value matches no attribute mapping for roles | **Refused** | **Removed** |
| An attribute mapping for roles matches, but names a role that doesn't exist in Bifrost | **Refused** | **Removed** |
| SCIM-only mode, and what SCIM last sent for the user resolves no role | **Refused** | **Kept, but not used.** The user can't sign in until SCIM sends data that resolves a role. |

When a role is **removed**:

- The access profiles that role gave the user are removed with it.
- Their teams, business units, projects, and other access profiles are still updated from the token, as on a successful sign-in.
- Their account is kept. Once your IdP sends a value that matches an attribute mapping for roles, their next sign-in gives them a role again.

Admins with **User Provisioning: Update** permission get a **Sign-in refused** notification explaining why, at most once an hour per user and reason.

To set up your first admin, map an IdP group or attribute to the Admin role before anyone signs in.

## How roles are decided

### From your attribute mappings for roles

Each attribute mapping for roles says "when attribute X has value Y, give role Z". When a user matches more than one mapping, your **role resolution** setting decides:

- **The role with the most permissions, ties by list order** (default) - someone matching both Viewer and Admin becomes Admin.
- **The first matching role, by list order** - the order of your mappings decides.

A mapping with the value `*` matches **any** value of that attribute. It is an ordinary mapping, not a fallback: it only matches when the attribute is present.

If you have **no attribute mappings for roles**, Bifrost reads the role claim your IdP sends and matches it to a Bifrost role of the same name (ignoring case).

### When your IdP changes a role

A user's role always follows your IdP. Each time your IdP sends new data for a user, Bifrost works out their role again from your attribute mappings for roles, and changes or removes the current role to match. A role is never locked in once it's set.

| What your IdP sends | At sign-in | Over SCIM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A value that matches an attribute mapping for roles | The user gets that role, **replacing** their current one | The user gets that role, **replacing** their current one |
| The attribute, with a value that matches no attribute mapping for roles | The role is **removed**, and the sign-in is refused | The role is **removed**. In SCIM only mode, the user's sign-ins are refused until SCIM sends a value that matches. In both mode, their next sign-in decides the role from the token. |
| Nothing about the role at all (attribute left out of a refreshed token) | The role is **kept** | - |
| Bifrost couldn't read your roles or the user's data | Nothing changes | Nothing changes |

When a role is removed:

- Access profiles given by that role are removed.
- If the user is left with no access profile at all, their virtual keys are removed too.
- Access profiles, teams, and business units from other sources are unaffected.

## Teams and business units

### Where a membership comes from

Bifrost records where each team and business unit membership came from, and shows it on the member as **Manual** or **Synced**:

| Source | Added by | Removed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manual** | An admin, in the dashboard or API | An admin. Syncs never remove it, unless your IdP takes it over (below). |
| **Sign-in claims** | A mapping rule matching a claim in the user's token | The next sign-in where the attribute is present and no longer matches |
| **SCIM user attribute** | A mapping rule matching an attribute pushed over SCIM | The next SCIM push where the attribute no longer matches. A sign-in can also remove it (below). |
| **SCIM group** | Your IdP adding the user to a pushed group | Your IdP removing the user from the group, or deleting the group. In **both** mode, a sign-in can also remove it (below). |

Things to know:

- **Admins can still add members in Bifrost.** A membership added in the dashboard or API is kept through every sync, so you can put people in teams and business units your IdP doesn't know about.
- **Your IdP takes over a membership it also grants.** If an admin adds someone to Platform and your IdP later grants Platform too, the membership becomes Synced and follows your IdP from then on, including removal.
- **Sign-in can remove SCIM memberships.** In **both** mode, a sign-in whose token sends a new value for a mapped attribute removes any SCIM membership that value no longer matches, whether it came from a SCIM user attribute or a SCIM group. In **SCIM only** mode, a sign-in re-applies your current rules to what SCIM last sent, so it removes SCIM user-attribute memberships only when you've changed your rules since the last push. SCIM group memberships are kept until your IdP changes them.
- **Missing teams and business units are created.** If a rule names a team or business unit that doesn't exist, it is created on first use.
- **Removing all your team rules** removes the memberships those rules created.

## Access profiles from your IdP

Access profile mapping rules **add** profiles. Every rule a user matches applies, and the user holds every profile they match **in addition to** the profiles from their role and any assigned by hand. See [Multiple profiles](/enterprise/access-profiles#multiple-profiles-what-a-user-can-do-and-who-pays).

- A profile the user still matches is left alone, keeping its budget and usage.
- A profile they stop matching is removed.
- If an admin assigned a profile by hand and a rule later grants it, the rule takes it over.

## When someone leaves

### What removes a user

| Event | SCIM Provisioning off | Both | SCIM only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SCIM deactivates the user (`active: false`) | - | Removed | Removed |
| SCIM deletes the user | - | Removed | Removed |
| A session refresh fails and your IdP reports the user inactive | Removed | Removed | Not removed |
| Background sync finds the user disabled, unassigned, or gone | Removed | - | - |
| An admin deletes the user in the dashboard or API | Removed | Removed | Removed |
| A sign-in is refused | **Not removed** | **Not removed** | **Not removed** |

A failed session refresh on its own never removes anyone. The user is signed out and asked to sign in again. Bifrost removes the user only when, after the refresh fails, it checks with your IdP's directory and your IdP reports the user as inactive or gone. If your IdP has no directory API, or the check fails, the user is kept.

### What removal does

When a user is removed, Bifrost:

- Removes their virtual keys and all their access profiles.
- Removes their team, business unit, and MCP memberships.
- Ends their dashboard and desktop-agent sessions.
- Removes their personal budget and rate limit.

Service accounts are never removed this way.

## Background sync and sessions

| Mechanism | How often | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sign-in** | Every sign-in | Full update. |
| **Session refresh** | When a session expires | Bifrost silently refreshes the session with your IdP and re-applies the token. |
| **Session sweep** | Every 24 hours | Refreshes sessions about to expire and removes users your IdP reports as gone. **Skipped when SCIM Provisioning is on.** |
| **Directory sync** | Every **Directory sync interval** (default 24 hours, from 1 hour to 30 days) | Re-reads users from your IdP's directory: creates, updates, and removes users. Needs directory API credentials. **Skipped when SCIM Provisioning is on.** |
| **SCIM push** | Real time | Whenever your IdP sends a change. |

<Warning>
	When SCIM Provisioning is on, the background jobs are off and the dashboard's **Sync users** and bulk import are unavailable. Your IdP must push every
	change, including removals. Check that your IdP's SCIM connector has deprovisioning turned on.
</Warning>

A refresh token can carry fewer claims than the original sign-in token. Because Bifrost remembers each attribute and only treats an attribute as changed when it is actually present, a slim refresh token never strips anyone's access.

### Which token Bifrost reads

- **Most providers** (Google, Okta, Entra, Auth0, generic OIDC): the **ID token**.
- **Keycloak**: the **access token**, because Keycloak puts its roles there.

## Applying rule changes to existing users

Mapping rules apply to each user at their next sign-in or SCIM push. To apply them to everyone now, save your rules and choose one of:

- **Reconcile existing users** - Re-evaluates every user against the new rules, using the data Bifrost stored at their last sign-in or SCIM push. In SCIM-only mode it uses what SCIM sent.
- **Sync users from your IdP** - Re-reads users from your IdP's directory (SCIM Provisioning off only). Best when stored data may be out of date.
- **Not now** - Users pick up the rules at their next sign-in or SCIM push.

Before running a reconcile:

- **Preview changes for existing users** shows a sample of users who would gain or lose a role, team, business unit, access profile, or project, including anyone who would lose every access profile and with it their keys.
- **Check a specific user** shows exactly what would change for one person.
- **Keep users with missing data unchanged** - When on, a user whose stored data lacks an attribute is left alone. When off (default), a missing attribute counts as empty, so whatever it granted is removed.

Reconcile skips service accounts, users an admin created by hand, and users with no stored data. A preview is tied to the rules it was made with, so if the rules change before you apply it, Bifrost asks you to preview again.

## Switching modes

| From → to | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| **SCIM Provisioning off → SCIM Provisioning on** | Background sync and dashboard imports stop. Your IdP's next push takes over memberships that came from sign-in. |
| **Both → SCIM only** | Sign-in stops reading the token's claims and re-applies your mapping rules to what SCIM last sent. Users created at sign-in can still sign in if what SCIM knows about them resolves a role. New users must be pushed first. |
| **SCIM only → both** | Sign-in drives access again. Users SCIM deactivated can be restored by signing in with a valid role. |

A reconcile running when the mode changes stops, and should be run again.

<Warning>
	Switching from **both** to **SCIM only** assumes your sign-in token and SCIM have been sending the same attributes and groups,
	with the same values, from the start. Bifrost doesn't merge the two: it stores the attributes from sign-in claims and overwrites
	them with the ones SCIM pushes. After the switch, roles, teams, and business units come only from that stored SCIM data. A user
	whose role came from a token claim that SCIM never sent is refused at their next sign-in, until SCIM sends a value that matches.
	Check that both carry the same data before you switch.
</Warning>

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| "Access denied" at sign-in | The user's token has no role attribute, or its value matches no attribute mapping for roles. Check the **Sign-in refused** notification and your attribute mappings for roles. |
| "Provisioning is managed by SCIM" | SCIM-only mode is on and your IdP hasn't pushed this user yet. Assign them to the Bifrost app in your IdP. |
| A user's role changed or was removed | Roles follow your IdP. Check what your IdP sends for the user, and which of your attribute mappings for roles it matches. |
| A user kept access after leaving a group | With SCIM Provisioning off, removal happens at their next sign-in or session refresh, or at the next background sync. With SCIM Provisioning on, check your IdP pushes group removals. |
| A team appeared that nobody created | A mapping rule named a team that didn't exist, so it was created. |
| Bulk import or **Sync users** is unavailable | SCIM Provisioning is on. Your IdP pushes users instead. |
